# Cascadeur rig animation (MCP + Python)

Everything here was learned the hard way on SimpleRPG Attack 1 (`I:/Art/Cascadeur/Longsword_Attack1_RightDiagonal.casc`). The details are in `references/`; this file is the workflow and the rules that prevent repeat mistakes.

## What this skill covers
- Driving Cascadeur via `mcp__cascadeur__run_script` (Python runs inside Cascadeur on the next scene-idle event).
- Posing the mixamorig rig by script: forward kinematics, IK, sword held rigidly in the hands.
- Verifying the result against the **evaluated scene**, not against the solver.
- Anatomical checks (wrist, forearm, elbow, torso clearance), blade edge/velocity checks.
- The export situation (licence) and the Unity Humanoid facts.

## First read these constraints (each one cost a failed attempt)
1. **Points drive joints.** The autoposing rig recomputes every joint orientation from rig points (`*_MainPoint`, `*_DirectionPoint`, `*_AdditionalPoint`, `*_Self0/1Point`, `*_LimbDir`). Writing joint `local_rotation` is overridden on update (feet ended 28-89 degrees off, arms up to 38). Pose by writing the **global_position of the points**, computed from your own joint globals plus the rigid offsets recorded at the Ready pose. Then joint positions land within 0.25 cm and hands match exactly.
2. **Arm and forearm share one roll; the hand takes all pronation.** `LeftArm` and `LeftForeArm` both use `LeftForeArm_AdditionalPoint` (ForeArm also `LeftForeArm_LimbDir`); rotating LimbDir 40 degrees about the forearm axis rolled Arm and ForeArm together by 25.8 each. Independent forearm twist cannot be authored in this rig as shipped. Solve with `twist_share = 0` so the solver equals the scene (0.0-0.1 degrees); with a non-zero share the scene differs by up to 38 degrees and every wrist number is wrong.
3. **Validate in the scene.** Solver and scene disagreed (solver 82 vs scene 101 degrees at the left wrist) until the model matched the rig. Always re-measure from `dv.get_data_value(id, frame)` after building.
4. **Key every frame.** Between sparse keys the rig re-derives hand orientation from interpolated points and spikes (wrist 129 degrees between keys 4 frames apart). Bake a key on every frame; use CLAMPED_BEZIER only if keys are sparse and you accept that.
5. **Cascadeur's free/trial licence refuses FBX export** ("only supports .CASC"). Do not try to bypass it; ask the user (upgrade, or a Blender route with their agreement).
6. **Scripts must finish in about 25 s** or the MCP call times out ("Timed out waiting for Cascadeur idle event"). Chunk long optimisation (about 20 frames x 25 trials per call); cache results to JSON between calls.
7. **`dv.union_get_data_value(id)` means frame 0.** Use `dv.get_data_value(id, frame)`. This trap produced a false "Ready pose is static" conclusion once.
8. **Do not name your own variables `X` or `Y`** in scripts that exec `posesolver.py`; they are the axis vectors and shadowing breaks `qaa(X, ...)` with a baffling dict error. Same for a local called `swing` (a function).
9. **The playback range resets** (`ns.animation_boundary().first_frame / .last_frame`) when a project is reloaded or rebuilt from a 2-frame file (0..100). Set it per clip and save, or playback stops mid-motion and looks like a bad ending.
10. **Check the grip before optimising motion.** The knuckle line of each hand should line up with the sword's true edge (measure the angle between `Index1 - Pinky1` in world, projected perpendicular to the blade, and the edge axis; about 0 or 180 degrees is aligned). The original Ready left hand was 39 degrees off, which forced extreme left-wrist twist/ulnar deviation in every cut and pushed the left elbow into the torso. Rolling a hand around the hilt is physically free; rolling the **left** hand +39 degrees (`roll_left` in `hand_pose`) fixed it and leaves the Unity weapon socket (right hand) untouched. Keep the right-hand relation to the sword unchanged unless the socket is re-exported.
11. **Random search is not enough; use a grid + dynamic programming for the hard frames.** For the cut, coarse grids over (sword roll, right swivel, left swivel) found feasible poses that 100+ random trials per frame missed (`gridopt.py`). Smooth the sword position/direction/torso nudges across frames before measuring the tip's travel direction, otherwise the edge-vs-travel angle jitters between 10 and 90 degrees from noise alone.
12. **Edge leading, comfortable wrists and torso clearance can conflict.** With a correct grip they coexist at the cost of larger forearm pronation (about 80-100 degrees relative to Ready). Check feasibility per frame by scanning roll and swivel before blaming the optimiser.
13. **Spikes hide in the base data, not only in the new offsets.** A 10-degree single-frame roll spike at frame 45 survived two rebuilds because the base vectors already contained it and despiking was applied only to the new offset table; the hand visibly jittered. Despike the final per-frame vectors themselves (`despike_vectors` pattern: replace a value that differs from the median of the four neighbours by more than a few times the typical second difference) and measure hand acceleration and hand rotation-rate change per frame (frames 40-50 should be under about 3 degrees/frame change).
14. **Quantised swivel paths snap the elbow.** A 24-degree swivel grid plus a weak transition penalty moved the right elbow 100 degrees in six frames (7 cm/frame). Limit the swivel step per frame (`dp_path(max_step=30, lam_s=0.03)`), add elbow and wrist jerk penalties to the per-frame cost (`trajopt7.JERK_W`), smooth the swivel columns in the recovery, and check elbow speed per frame (a spike above about 3 cm/frame after a quiet stretch is a snap).
15. **Flat-first leading is a timing problem as much as a roll problem.** Right after the hit the blade still moves 5-12 m/s sideways (torso rotation carries the tip along the sword's flat) and the left forearm cannot twist enough to bring the edge round. Giving the optimiser a stronger edge weight only traded flat frames for limit violations. What worked: re-time the post-hit path so the blade decelerates smoothly (`warp7.build_warp2`: arclength schedule 16, 14, 12, 10, 8.6, 7.2, 6, 5, 4.1, 3.4, 2.8 m/s ... to an overswing extreme at about frame 84, then the old recovery), so speed is under about 3 m/s before the geometry becomes infeasible. The residue was finally cleared by allowing forearm twist up to 110 degrees (user agreed), torso yaw (rule 22) and a user-posed anchor (rule 23); ask early whether more twist is acceptable.
16. **Torso twist can reduce wrist demand, but only in the cut.** Extra torso yaw with the sword's world pose held fixed (`trajopt7.apply_off7` compensates `sw_off`, `sw_dir` and roll so the blade does not move; the sword position shifts about 1.5 cm because the shoulder centre moves) cut the left twist at frame 68 from -84 to -58 degrees as the yaw extra rose from 0 to 30 degrees. In the overswing the same extra yaw cost elbow clearance (35 to 14 cm) without lowering twist, so use a profile: about +12 at frame 62, +24 at 69-73, back to 0 by 78. A random search never discovered this (extras stayed near 0); a direct scan over the torso yaw value per frame (`ty7_chunk.py`) did.
17. **Time-warping beats re-optimising for timing changes.** The warped base vectors keep the previously solved roll and swivels, so only a short regrid of the changed frames is needed.
18. **The Unity MCP may be attached to the wrong project** (it was Bugshooter, not SimpleRPG). Check `unity_editor_ping`; do not modify other projects.
19. **Overswing size drives forearm twist; reach limits the 'pull away'.** Shrinking the excursion past the hit (`overswing8.shape_overswing`, alpha 0.8) cut left twist about 7 degrees. Pushing the sword outward along the chest-to-grip line only works up to the arm's reach: raise `reach_frac` (grip dict, default 0.955; 0.975 used in v8) and expect elbow bend near 26 degrees at the extreme. Re-solve roll/swivel with grid + DP after reshaping, since those columns are not scaled.
20. **A wobbling tip path comes from linear interpolation in the time warp, not from the optimiser offsets.** Check tip speed and per-frame direction change (`metrics9.metrics9`) before and after any retime. Smooth the pose columns and re-time with `retime9.smooth_retime` (monotone speed decay, Catmull-Rom sampling). Smooth roll and swivel columns afterwards ([1 2 1]) because a 24-degree swivel grid creates elbow snaps.
21. **Raising the elbow (swivel) is not a free fix for flat leading.** At the post-hit pose the left arm trades forearm twist against clearance and ulnar deviation (see v9 notes in attack1-results.md); an elbow-up cost term did not change the result.
22. **Torso yaw with the sword held fixed is the lever for left-arm twist and the end-of-swing flat blade.** Positive extra torso yaw (character-left shoulder goes back) cut left twist about 10 degrees and let a sword roll of +25 make the back edge lead at the overswing, once the twist allowance was raised to 110. Apply it with `gridopt9.apply_yaw` before the roll/swivel grid, and scale the DP edge table (x10) where the lead rule is gated off by low speed.
23. **To build around a pose the user hand-edited in Cascadeur, read it from the scene, not from your parameters.** `build11.build_v11` inverts the sword's world pose into torso-frame parameters, time-maps the old path (monotone speed, sword may need a later stopping frame) and blends the residual in. If the anchor sits at a different phase than the old path the speed profile cannot be monotone unless the stopping frame moves later.
24. **Run `review12.review` on the evaluated scene after every build, over ALL frames.** It flags joint position/rotation spikes, hard-limit wrist frames, torso clearance and blade-to-body distance. Swivel snaps hide wherever a DP window ends or a pinned frame sits between grid-quantised neighbours (24-degree steps); smooth roll/swivel columns after any DP or pin, and check the 3 frames either side of every window edge.
25. **Reverse cuts cannot keep the true edge leading from the same grip** (details in `references/attack2-results.md`): the feasible sword roll window is about -60..+30 degrees, so a retraced path leads with the back edge. Test feasibility with a roll scan at 2-3 hit frames before building a whole clip. Pelvis yaw and hip translation are free for the arms and are the right tools for windup body motion; match the previous clip's end velocity with a Hermite ease (initial slope) so the seam is continuous in motion, not just in pose.
26. **Re-using a solved clip in reverse is a fast base for the next attack** (`attack2_base.py`: `ArcPath` arclength re-timing, `bell_profile`, Hermite ease). Copy the finished project file to the new name and let `build.build` clear and re-key it.
27. **Designed key poses are usually anatomically infeasible; polish each key, chained in time order** (`keypolish.py`, details and traps in `references/attack3-results.md`): keep blade-direction nudges at 0 so the aim line holds, add a continuity term so consecutive keys stay on one swivel branch, limit grip draw-in with `zmax`, then rebuild the base through the polished vectors and run the grid + DP. Torso extras with the sword world pose held are how the arms get comfortable without moving the blade.
28. **Layout every attack as Attack / Combo continuation / Neutral recovery with ONE shared branch pose** (`references/clip-ranges.md`). The combo track must equal the neutral track up to the end of the active hit (`branches.fix_combo_branch`: copy, then fade the old difference out so the combo END pose stays pinned to the next attack's first frame); verify joint equality on the evaluated scene at the branch frame. A time re-map (`branches.retime_attack_ease`, or arclength `ArcPath` + `bell_profile`) moves boundaries without rebuilding the movement. Zero-tangent keys and Hermite eases create near-stationary stretches (stalls): check tip speed per frame for runs below about 0.3 m/s inside a stroke.
29. **`build.build` removes the current scene if its name starts with `Longsword_Attack1` or `untitled`, and saving fails if an older tab of the same file is open**: remove stale tabs of the target file first; cross-scene comparisons need both tabs open, so compare vectors instead.

## Workflow that worked
1. **Work dir**: pick a scratch folder, copy `scripts/*.py` there, set `SP = "<dir>/"` (forward slashes, trailing slash).
2. **Dump rig data** once from the canonical Ready pose scene: `exec(open(SP+"dump_rig_data.py").read())` writes `skel.json`, `points.json`.
3. **Load order** (exec into one namespace): `session6.py` loads everything for optimisation sessions (set `SP`, `GRIP_FILE` and `FINAL_FILE` first); chunk runners: `chunk7.py` (random polish with torso/pelvis twist), `grid7_chunk.py` (roll x swivel grid, `CLIP='combo'` for the Combo clip), `ty7_chunk.py` (per-frame torso-yaw scan), `warp7.py` (post-hit re-timing); `writer.py, writer2.py, viz.py, analysis.py, build.py, validate.py` are for building and checking the project. `attack1_v3.py` is the Attack 1 design (key poses as torso yaw/pitch/roll, sword grip position in torso frame, blade direction, lheel); copy and edit it for new attacks. `attack1_final_vecs13.json` (older: 6-11) and `attack1_grip8.json` are the final Attack 1 per-frame parameter vectors and the grip (`reach_frac` 0.975); `attack1_user_pose_f80.json` is the user's hand-posed sword at frame 80. Never name your own variables or helper functions `X`, `Y`, `swing` or `S` (see rule 8; I hit `S` as a helper function name, which silently broke `solve`); `session6` code uses `XS` for the offset table.
4. **Design keys**: few key poses per clip, expressed in the torso frame (sword position and blade direction relative to Spine2). Mark the hit window and combo/neutral branches (Neutral clip frames 0-130, Combo clip frames 140+).
5. **Per-key refine** (`run_refine`, `opt5`): random local search over roll, elbow swivel, small sword nudges, with continuity to the previous key (older approach; still useful to get design keys).
6. **Curve + per-frame optimise**: `interp.make_curves` builds Hermite curves through the refined keys. Then, with `session6`: `trajopt6.sweep6` (random polish; costs = anatomical comfort + torso clearance + edge lead + smoothness, priority hard limits > clearance > comfort > edge), `gridopt.stageA` + `dp_path` + `apply_path` for the hard frames (cut, overswing, recovery; about 3.8 s per frame per grid, 5 frames per MCP call), `comfort.py` for limits. Smooth the nudge columns (3-10) afterwards.
7. **Build** (`build.build(path, params_by_frame, total_frames)`): reload the saved project, extend the timeline (clears all keys, leaves stray keys at 1 and N which `build` removes), write point keys for every frame (`writer2.write_keys2`), set interpolation, then `analysis.bake_sword(range)` keys the sword from the right hand every frame. Save with `scene.save(path)`.
8. **Validate in the scene** with `validate.py` (`validate_scene(ds, frames, label)` reports wrist components, clearance, edge angle, blade speed, grip slide, feet, head; plus Ready-pose consistency). Report numbers; do not claim success from solver output.

## Recommended pipeline for a new attack (distilled from Attack 1 v1 to v13)
Order matters; each step fixed a class of flaw the user later noticed by eye.
1. **Grip first** (rule 10), then **design keys** (`attack1_v3.py` pattern) in the torso frame with the lead rule in mind: the true edge leads the whole cut, either edge may lead afterwards, never the flat while the tip moves faster than about 3 m/s.
2. **Time map before optimising.** Build the tip path with a monotone speed profile (`retime9.smooth_retime`; floor speed for a combo clip that must keep moving) and sample with Catmull-Rom, never linear. Check `metrics9.metrics9`: no tip direction change above about 25 degrees per frame, speed non-increasing after the peak.
3. **Torso yaw extras** (`gridopt9.apply_yaw` profiles) before the roll/swivel grid, with sword world pose compensated (rule 22).
4. **Roll/swivel grid + DP** (`grid9_chunk.py`, about 6 frames per MCP call, `TWIST=(60,110)` once the user allows more twist, `W_TRUE`/`W_EITHER` for lead weights, scale the DP edge table x4-x10 where low speed gates the lead rule off, anchor the window ends), then `dp_path`, `apply_path9`.
5. **Smooth roll/swivel columns** after the DP and after pinning any frame ([1 2 1] passes or Gaussian sigma 2). Grid quantisation produces elbow snaps (rule 24).
6. **Build, bake the sword, `validate.validate_scene`, then `review12.review` over all frames.** Report numbers honestly, including frames past a limit.
7. **Show the user contact sheets** (`viz.render_png` every 6th frame with a tip trail); hand-edited poses from the user override the solver (rule 23).
8. **Back up before overwriting** the project (`_backup_vN.casc`), set `animation_boundary` 0-130, save, and record the version in `references/attack1-results.md` and memory.
Time per attack: grid chunks dominate (about 20 s per 6 frames per clip); budget 8-10 calls for a 50-frame window.

## Validation checklist (run on the evaluated scene)
- Wrist: decompose `rel = inv(Q_forearm) * Q_hand` into twist about local Y and swing; split swing into flexion/extension and radial/ulnar deviation using the knuckle line (`Index1 - Pinky1` = radial direction). Report each, not just the combined magnitude (an "82 degree swing" hid 82 degrees of ulnar deviation). Typical limits: flexion 80, extension 70, radial 20, ulnar 35, pronation/supination about 85 each way. Prefer comfortable working ranges in the strike (about 60-70% of those), not the extremes.
- Elbow bend not locked (keep above about 25-35 degrees) and no per-frame jump above about 10 degrees in swing or twist. Hand jitter: per-frame hand rotation-rate change under about 3 degrees. Elbow snap: elbow speed with no isolated spike above about 3 cm/frame in the recovery.
- Flat-first leading: list frames where the angle between the tip's travel and the flat normal is below 35 degrees while the tip moves faster than 3 m/s; there should be none in the cut and overswing.
- **Body clearance**: distance of elbow, mid-forearm and wrist from the spine polyline (Hips, Spine, Spine1, Spine2, Neck); the torso is about 18-20 cm half-thickness, so keep the elbow centre at 22 cm or more and the mid-forearm at 20 cm or more. Before the fix the left elbow was 11.6 cm (frame 72) and 1.3 cm (frame 86) from the axis.
- **Edge alignment**: angle between the true edge (sword joint local -X) and the tip's travel direction projected perpendicular to the blade; under 20 degrees through the hit window. Flat leading (travel along local Y) looks wrong immediately.
- Grip stability: hand-to-sword distance constant (sword is baked from the right hand with a fixed offset).
- Blade speed profile: peak in the middle of the hit window (Attack 1: 25 m/s at frame 59), windup never stalls.
- Feet: planted foot position range, heel lift only on the rear foot.
- Head stays within a few degrees of forward (first-person camera on the head).
- End pose equals the Ready pose (compare frame 130 and the Combo clip's frame 0 against frame 0; use the NEW Ready if the grip changed); the playback range (`animation_boundary`) covers the clip.

## Units and conventions
Centimetres; character faces +Z; character's left is +X; Y up. Blade is local -Z of `Longsword_01` (109.44 cm to `Longsword_02`), edge axis local X (-X is the true edge, facing forward at Ready), flat normal local Y. `Longsword_01` is a root joint (parent none); it is not parented to the hand, so key it every frame from `RightHand` with a constant offset.
